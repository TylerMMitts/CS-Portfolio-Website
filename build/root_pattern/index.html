Why roots

Roots do more than anchor a plant. They control how much carbon it sends underground, how much soil it can reach, and how well it takes up water and nutrients, while also shaping the microbial community living around them, making root structure a key factor in drought tolerance, nutrient efficiency, and soil health.

Across large populations of maize roots, this project has found that cortical cell size follows a strikingly consistent pattern moving outward from the stele, a “cortical cell pattern” that appears to be a fundamental feature of root anatomy rather than a random byproduct of growth. This pattern holds not just within maize but across multiple species, suggesting it may be a universal feature of root structure, one worth understanding for what it could reveal about the traits that matter most for crop resilience.
